### What is left, decomposed into two error terms

**E1 — finite degeneration loop gain.** Each branch is a `VIN`-referenced
PMOS degenerated by `Rgm` = 200 kΩ (`design/ldo_softstart.sch`,
`Rgma_ss`/`Rgmb_ss`), so its small-signal transconductance is

```
Gm_branch = gm / (1 + gm*Rgm) = (1/Rgm) * [ 1 / (1 + 1/(gm*Rgm)) ]
```

i.e. low by `delta = 1/(1 + gm*Rgm)` and **never high**. Four independent
checks in the committed data say this is the dominant term:

1. **One-sided.** `I_inj(0)` is low at 63/63 corners (−16.9% … −3.2%). A
   finite-`gm*Rgm` error can only be low; the pre-cascode ΔV_ds error was
   two-sided (+5.32 … +8.88 µA against 6.00, i.e. −11% … +48%), which is why
   DR-0023's fix changed the error's *shape* and not only its size.
2. **It is a gain error with a nearly correct zero-crossing.** At the 21
   minimum-supply corners the `I_inj(0)` error (9.0 … 16.9% low) and the
   transconductance error (11.0 … 17.1% low) track each other to within
   **2.48 percentage points**, and the implied linear zero-crossing
   `I_inj(0)/|Gm|` is **1.192 … 1.234 V** — within −0.6% / +2.9% of `VREF`.
   The element is a clean linear transconductor whose slope is short, not a
   mis-aimed one.
3. **It falls with temperature.** Implied `gm*Rgm` at `tt_*_2.97v`:
   **7.36 / 6.89 / 6.30** at −40 / 27 / 125 °C, the direction mobility sets.
4. **It rises with supply.** Each branch carries
   `Ia = (VIN − V(gate) − |Vsg|)/Rgm`, so more supply is more bias is more
   `gm`: `delta` at `tt_27c_*` is **12.7% / 10.1% / 7.5%** at
   2.97 / 3.30 / 3.63 V.

Implied `gm*Rgm` spans **4.91** (`sf_125c_2.97v`) to **30.0**
(`ff_-40c_3.63v`); at `Rgm` = 200 kΩ that is `gm` = 25 … 150 µS, consistent
with a 4 µm/1 µm `pfet_03v3` carrying ~10 µA (`design/ldo_softstart.sch`,
SIZING AS BUILT).

**E2 — soft rectification near `VREF`.** The gap between the `I_inj(0)` error
and the slope error grows monotonically with supply: **−0.54 … +2.48 pp** at
the 2.97 V corners, **+0.68 … +7.59 pp** at 3.30 V, **+2.10 … +24.72 pp** at
3.63 V. It is co-located with the residual (T1: worst **1433 nA** at
`ff_125c_3.63v`) and the delayed release (T4: worst **2.025 V**, same corner).
This is precisely what `design/ldo_softstart.sch`'s "WHERE THE CASCODE'S
HEADROOM COMES FROM" section predicts and says is visible in the per-corner
hand-over transfer: `GMSUM` is `Mgmb_ss`'s drain, and at the high-supply,
low-mirror-current end it rises back toward `VREF + |Vtp|` and `Mgmb_ss`
re-enters triode.

**Mapping**: E1 sets T2 and T3; E2 sets T1 and T4.

### The one quantity that fails a ratified row, and its identity

`README.md`'s Startup row (as amended by DR-0024, **ratified** 2026-09-15 via
PR #228) bounds inrush at ≤ 5 mA and current-limit clearance at ≥ 10 mA below
62.0 mA, both at `C_eff` = 1 µF. Measured cascoded: **0/83 and 2/83**, worst
**314.925 mA**. #246 §3 attributes this to the hold-release acquisition step
and measures the mechanism (same-binary A/B; ideal-ramp A/B ruling the ramp
node out at −9.0% … +12.6%).

That step has a closed form, and it is worth writing out because it decides
the first alternative below. With `FB` pinned at `VREF`, KCL at `FB` gives
`I_inj = VREF/Rbot + (VREF − VOUT)/Rtop`, so

```
VOUT(V(SSR)=0) = VREF − Rtop*( I_inj(0) − VREF/Rbot )
```

and substituting the measured shortfall
`I_inj(0) = (1 − delta_eff) * VREF * (1/Rtop + 1/Rbot)`, written out term by
term to leave no room for a sign slip:

```
VOUT(V(SSR)=0) = VREF − Rtop*VREF*[ (1−delta_eff)/Rtop + (1−delta_eff)/Rbot − 1/Rbot ]
               = VREF − VREF*(1−delta_eff) + VREF*delta_eff*(Rtop/Rbot)
               = delta_eff * VREF * (1 + Rtop/Rbot)
               = delta_eff * 1.8 V
```

**`Rtop` and `Rbot` cancel identically.** The acquisition step is
`1.8 V × delta_eff` and does not contain the feedback transimpedance at all.
With `delta_eff` = 3.225 … 16.917% from `targets.csv`, that is
**58.1 … 304.5 mV**, reproducing #246 §3's independently stated +58 … +305 mV
exactly. The worst corner is `sf_125c_2.97v` — **minimum** supply, i.e. E1,
not E2.

## Decision (proposed)

**Make the injection transconductance accurate by closing a local feedback
loop around the degenerated branches, so `Gm` is set by the
`Rgm : Rtop‖Rbot` resistor ratio rather than by the open-loop degeneration
factor `gm*Rgm/(1 + gm*Rgm)`.** Concretely: a local amplifier forces each
branch's degeneration node to track its own gate reference (`V(SSR)` and
`VREF` respectively), so the branch current becomes `(VIN − V_gate)/Rgm` to
within the amplifier's own error, instead of `(VIN − V_gate − |Vsg|)/Rgm`
with `|Vsg|` moving against the signal.

**A second, separable requirement of the same decision**: `Mgmb_ss` must be
held in **saturation** across the whole PVT grid, so `GMSUM` stays below
`VREF + |Vtp|` at the high-supply, low-mirror-current end. This is E2, it is
a bias-headroom constraint rather than a sizing preference, and it is not
fixed as a side effect of E1's servo.

Everything DR-0023 ratified is **kept unchanged**: the cascoded mirror
(`Mgmr_ss`/`Mgmrc_ss` against `Mgmo_ss`/`Mgmc_ss`), rectification by mirror
cutoff rather than by a comparator, and the element's single point of contact
with the main loop (`Mgmc_ss`'s drain on `FB`).

**This record ratifies the topology, not the sizing.** The amplifier's
structure, bias and compensation are the implementation's to choose against
the constraints below, exactly as DR-0023 left `Mgmc_ss`/`Mgmrc_ss`'s sizing
to #246.

### What this must achieve, and what it costs

**Against T1–T4.** With the local loop closed at gain `A`, the residual is
`delta = 1/(A*(1 + gm*Rgm))`. At the worst measured `gm*Rgm` of 4.91 and a
modest `A` = 40 dB, `delta` ≤ **0.17%** — **10× inside T2's ±1.7%** — and the
binding term becomes the `Rgm : Rtop‖Rbot` ratio. E2's separate requirement
is what carries T1 and T4.

**This trades a `gm`-limited error for a resistor-ratio error, and that trade
must be declared, not hidden.** `README.md` note 3 records that this PDK's
resistor models hard-code local mismatch to zero and publish no mismatch
coefficient for `ppolyf_u_3k` (`sim/devchar/CONCLUSIONS.md` §2), so the ratio
term **cannot be obtained from simulation against these models** and is
carried as a stated assumption. The cell already depends on this ratio —
`design/ldo_softstart.sch` says so ("a RATIO to the divider … the ratio, not
the absolute value, is the property being preserved") — so the assumption is
not new; what changes is that it stops being negligible next to a 9–17% `gm`
term and becomes the dominant one. The implementing record must state it as
an assumption and must not report a simulated `delta` ≈ 0 as evidence that T2
is met in silicon.

**Against the ratified `< 30 µA` Iq row** (DR-0004 amendment A6;
`sim/quiescent-current/records/20260918-190801-8a59d23.md`, PASS 81/81). The
row binds **at no load and at full load**, and the two clauses do not leave
the same headroom at the binding `ff_125c_3.63v` corner:

| clause | measured at `ff_125c_3.63v` | headroom to 30 µA |
|---|---|---|
| `iq_en_ua` (no load) | 28.0411 µA | 1.96 µA |
| `iq_full_ua` (full load) | **28.71 µA** | **1.29 µA** |

#249, #246 §8 and `design/ldo_softstart.sch` all quote the 1.96 µA figure.
**The binding number for anything that adds standing current is 1.29 µA**,
and this record uses that. A servo amplifier is a new standing branch, so
this is *the* constraint on the implementation. It is a DC and ms-scale servo
with no bandwidth requirement of consequence, so a single-stage amplifier at
a few hundred nA is the target shape; the implementation must **measure and
attribute** its adder rather than estimate it (DR-0023's Iq estimate was half
right and #246 had to correct it by +2.7 µA).

If it does not fit in 1.29 µA, there is a second budget available. The
element's whole Iq adder over the ideal `Binj_ss` baseline is **+7.18 µA** at
that same corner, and `design/ldo_softstart.sch` attributes it: "Both
degenerated branches carry `(VIN - |Vsg| - V(gate))/200 kohm` and neither
switches off at hand-over — only the mirror rectifies — so the element stands
~2 × Ia of DC current forever." Since the mirror is off in the settled state
(the −1.78 … −0.50 mV settled error is 5.9 … 1.7 nA through `Rtop`),
essentially all of that +7.18 µA is standing branch current that serves no
purpose after hand-over. Recovering it — by switching the branches off once
the element has released — is a real second budget and is filed separately as
out of this record's scope.

**Against the ratified ±2% accuracy row.** Unchanged, and untouched by
construction: the element rectifies off in the settled state (settled error
−1.78 … −0.50 mV, PASS by 20×), so the servo is not conducting when that row
is measured.

**Against the main loop's phase and gain margin.** Unchanged by construction.
The element's only contact with the main loop is `Mgmc_ss`'s drain on `FB`,
and this decision adds nothing to that node: the servo sits on `GMSA`/`GMSB`,
inside the element. #246 §5 re-measured the cascoded element at **ΔPM
0.00 deg / ΔGM 0.00 dB** against T6's 2 deg / 1 dB bar, with `FB`'s own
capacitance budget at 3 pF (hold-release state) / 10 pF (sink state) against
the element's tens of femtofarads. There is no mechanism in this decision by
which T6 or T7 can move — but they must be **re-measured**, not assumed, the
same discipline #246 applied rather than quoting DR-0023's numbers.

**The new local loop carries its own stability obligation**, and three
constraints bound it:

- It must sense the **branch** node (`GMSA`/`GMSB`), never `FB`. The
  injection element staying a one-way current source into `FB` is the
  structural argument `design/softstart_injection_compensation.md` §1 rests
  T6 on; a servo that closed through `FB` would destroy it.
- Its unity-gain frequency must sit well below the main loop's crossover, so
  the two cannot interact.
- It must be stable across the branch-bias swing the ramp traverses — `Ia`
  runs from `(VIN − |Vsg|)/Rgm` at `V(SSR)` = 0 down to cutoff at hand-over,
  roughly a decade. This is the hard part of the design and must be verified
  at every PVT corner, not assumed from a nominal-corner result.

## Alternatives considered

- **Scale the feedback transimpedance** — `Rtop`/`Rbot` and the injection
  current together, R4's first named lever. **Rejected on two independent
  grounds, the first of which is decisive on its own.**

  1. **It is exactly scale-invariant against the failure.** The identity in
     "Context" has `Rtop` and `Rbot` cancelling: the acquisition step is
     `1.8 V × delta_eff`, with no transimpedance in it. And `delta_eff` is
     itself invariant under the scaling: taking `Rgm → Rgm/k`, `W → k*W`,
     `I → k*I` leaves the branch overdrive `V_ov = sqrt(2I/(µCox*W/L))`
     unchanged, so `gm = 2I/V_ov` scales by `k` and **`gm*Rgm` is exactly
     preserved**. A 10× divider therefore leaves the 58 … 305 mV step, and
     the inrush failure, bit-for-bit where they are.

     R4's "buys a 10× relaxation on every target above" is a **unit
     illusion**: T1 and T2 were themselves *derived* from the ±36 mV voltage
     budget through `Rtop` (50 nA = 15 mV / 300 kΩ; ±0.10 µA =
     ±30 mV / 300 kΩ), so scaling `Rtop` scales the targets and the measured
     errors by the same factor and the pass counts stay at 1/63 and 0/63.
     Holding T1/T2 at their present absolute numbers while scaling `Rtop`
     would be relaxing the ratified ±2% allocation by 10× without saying so —
     which is the thing CLAUDE.md forbids.

  2. **Iq, and the cost is now ~4.5× worse than R4's own estimate.** R4
     costed a 10× divider at +18 µA (divider only: 2 → 20 µA), against a then
     ~5 µA of headroom. That estimate **predates #191's device-level
     transconductor**, whose branches are a *standing* DC current — the
     +7.18 µA element adder measured at the binding corner — and which scale
     with `k` as well. A 10× scaling therefore costs
     `2*(k−1) + 7.18*(k−1)` = `9.18*9` ≈ **+83 µA** against **1.29 µA** of
     full-load headroom. Solving `9.18*(k−1) ≤ 1.29` gives **k ≤ 1.14**
     (`k ≤ 1.21` even on the looser 1.96 µA no-load figure) — and by (1),
     `k` = 1.14 buys nothing.

- **Reinstate an output-side clamp on the hold-release hand-over** — R4's
  second named lever. **Rejected as the primary lever; retained as a named
  contingency** (see Consequences).

  - It does not remove the error, it manages the rate at which the loop
    acquires it. The 58 … 305 mV pedestal remains, so the block's own
    contract (`VOUT = 1.5 * V(SSR)`, `design/ldo_softstart.sch`'s first line)
    is still violated by up to 17% of full scale at the bottom of the ramp,
    and T1–T4 stay exactly where they are.
  - **The rate arithmetic is feasible but buys nothing else.** Acquiring
    305 mV into 1 µF while holding `icap_peak` ≤ 5 mA needs ≥ 61 µs of
    hand-over, and the same 61 µs is what holds peak `dVOUT/dt` ≤ 5 V/ms.
    Against DR-0024's ratified 1.8 ms settling clause (worst measured
    `t_startup` 1.28724 ms, so ~0.51 ms of slack) that fits — it spends ~12%
    of the remaining settling margin to hide an error the chosen lever
    removes.
  - **It puts a second element on `PASS_GATE`**, the node `Cm_ss`/`Rz_ss`
    were removed from. In its historical `#38`/`#43` shape — a comparator on
    (`FB`, `SSR`) driving a PMOS into `PASS_GATE` — it is a second feedback
    loop with its own compensation question, which R1 and #196/PR #197 have
    twice declined to reopen, and it is **~11 800 µm²**: about 12% of the
    ratified 0.1 mm² core-area row on its own, against a single small
    amplifier added to a 42 µm² cell. A *non-regulating* gate-slew limiter is
    far cheaper and would end in cutoff like `Mhold_ss` (≈ 0 settled Iq), but
    it is a rate limiter, not a fix, and it leaves every target in the table
    above failing.

- **A seventh sizing iteration of the `Mgm*` transconductor** — out of scope
  per #249, and the arithmetic says why, which is worth recording so "just
  bias it harder" is closed with a number rather than an assertion.
  `delta = 1/(1 + gm*Rgm) ≤ 1.7%` requires **`gm*Rgm ≥ 58`**, against a worst
  measured 4.91. A pure device scaling cannot deliver it (alternative 1(1)'s
  invariance argument). Raising `gm` at fixed `Rgm` = 200 kΩ requires
  `gm ≥ 290 µS`, and the **weak-inversion ceiling** — the most
  transconductance any MOS can produce at a given current — is
  `gm = Ia/(n*kT/q)`. At the binding hot corner,
  `Ia ≈ (2.97 − 0.8)/200 kΩ ≈ 10.9 µA` and `n*kT/q ≈ 1.4 × 34.3 mV`, giving
  `gm_max ≈ 226 µS`, i.e. **`gm*Rgm` tops out near 45 — short of the 58
  required, with no margin, at the theoretical limit of the device.** Raising
  `Ia` instead is closed by the Iq row long before it gets there. (These two
  figures are analytic bounds with `n ≈ 1.4` assumed, not simulated results;
  they are stated to show the lever is off by a structural margin rather than
  by a tuning margin.)

- **Add a `Cm_ss`/`Rz_ss`-class compensation network, or bandwidth-limit
  `GMSUM`** — already ruled out by R1 and #196/PR #197's negative result, and
  re-confirmed by #246 §5 against the cascoded element at 0.00 deg / 0.00 dB.
  Not reconsidered here.

- **Trim the injection current** — not available. This part is specified
  untrimmed (DR-0005's "untrimmed window" framing; `README.md`'s accuracy
  notes), and introducing a trim to meet a soft-start-only target would be a
  far larger spec change than the one this record makes.

## Consequences

- **The follow-on implementation PR's scope, gated on ratification**: this
  record's Status line flipped from `proposed` to `ratified <merge date>`; the
  servo topology in `design/ldo_softstart.sch` plus the E2 bias-headroom fix;
  the `design/softstart_injection_compensation.md` §3 pointer back to this
  record (in the same "Update (issue …)" style §1 and §4 already use, keeping
  R4's two-lever framing as history and correcting its "10× relaxation"
  arithmetic and its +18 µA Iq figure by reference to this record's
  Alternatives rather than rewriting them in place); a 163-point
  `sim/soft-start` re-run and the 63-corner hand-over transfer superseding
  `20260918-190207-8a59d23`; a `sim/quiescent-current` re-run with the adder
  measured and attributed against the **1.29 µA full-load** headroom; T6 and
  T7 re-measured against the new element; and SIZING AS BUILT updated with
  the as-built area and the declared resistor-ratio assumption. **No
  `design/` change lands with this record.**

- **The residual risk, stated rather than assumed.** `delta` → 0 is
  *necessary* for the hand-over; the evidence that it is *sufficient* is one
  generation old in the ramp sizing. The ideal `Binj_ss` element
  (`delta` = 0 exactly) measured `icap_peak` **0.3685 … 0.8023 mA at 63/63**
  and 0.4542 … 2.7890 mA at 20/20 for `C_eff` = 1 µF
  (`sim/soft-start/records/20260906-202950-bfc4a0a.md`, inrush section) — but
  at the **pre-DR-0024** ramp sizing. DR-0024's 5× `Css`/`Ch_ss`/`Cr_ss` cut
  costs about 4× on its own at the nominal corner
  (`20260915-103035-18f664f.md`: 1.195 → 4.760 mA at `tt_27c_3.30v`, same
  element, resize only). 0.80 mA × 4 ≈ 3.2 mA against a 5 mA bound is inside,
  but not comfortably, and no measurement exists of `delta` = 0 at DR-0024's
  sizing. **If the follow-on lands T2 and inrush still fails at 1 µF, the
  next addition is the rejected alternative's *non-regulating* hand-over rate
  limiter on `PASS_GATE` — not a regulating clamp.** This record's rejection
  of the clamp is a rejection as the *primary* lever only.

- **#187 and #212**: this decision sits upstream of both and is expected to
  close their remaining inrush and current-limit-clearance items through the
  mechanism above, but **it does not itself close them** and must not be
  cited as if it did — only a `sim/soft-start` re-run can. #187 additionally
  concerns the 4.7 µF end of DR-0001's window, which DR-0022 already restated
  as characterized rather than bound above 1 µF.

- **The Iq row becomes the binding constraint on the implementation**, not on
  this decision, and this record tightens the number the implementation must
  design to: **1.29 µA at full load**, not the 1.96 µA no-load figure #249
  and #246 §8 quote. The servo has to fit inside it or be paired with the
  branch-current recovery named above.

- **Area is not a constraint here**: one amplifier of the same
  small-geometry class as the cell's existing eight 4 µm² devices, against a
  transconductor that is currently under 0.2% of the ratified 0.1 mm²
  core-area row. The rejected clamp, at ~11 800 µm², would have been.

- **What gets harder**: the element acquires a second loop. Until now its
  whole appeal was that it needed no comparator, no timer and no second loop
  — it disengaged because its own argument went through zero. That property
  survives (the servo is inside the element and does not change how it
  rectifies), but "no second loop anywhere in this cell" does not, and the
  servo's stability across a decade of branch bias is new verification work
  that did not exist before.
