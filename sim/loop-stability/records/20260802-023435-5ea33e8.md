## Added by hand: the 57-point verdict delta against the superseded record

Everything above this section was written by
`sim/loop-stability/testbench/sweep.py`. This section was added by the same
run's author, before the record was committed, and reports the point-by-point
comparison against `20260801-191742-84f67b8` that the acceptance criteria of
issue #55 ask for. Nothing above it was altered.

**Headline: 2632/3240 pass here against 2689/3240 there — 57 points changed
verdict, all in the same direction, and no point changed the other way.** That
is a regression *by the recorded metric*, and it is reported as one. What
follows is the attribution, which is that the metric moved and the loop did
not.

The 57 points have four properties in common, all checkable from the two
matrix CSVs:

1. **All 57 are gain-margin-only.** No point in the matrix newly fails on
   phase margin. Across the 57, phase margin moves by **≤ 0.5 °** (e.g.
   `fs_-40c_3.63v` / 0.1 mA / 0.33 µF / 1 mΩ: 71.71 ° → 71.24 °).
2. **All 57 had `gain_margin_db = inf` in the superseded record** — meaning
   "the dec-50 grid found no −180 ° crossing anywhere in 0.01 Hz … 1 GHz",
   not "the margin is large".
3. **They occupy four PVT corners, all at Vin = 3.63 V** (`fs_-40c_3.63v` 21,
   `ss_-40c_3.63v` 12, `ss_27c_3.63v` 12, `tt_-40c_3.63v` 12), and only the
   0.1 mA and 1 mA load columns — the light-load region DR-0007 already scopes
   out.
4. **The superseded record's own rows already flagged them.** At those points
   the `f0_rising_hz` field is populated in the *superseded* raw logs (e.g.
   `fs_-40c_3.63v`, 0.1 mA / 0.33 µF / 1 mΩ: `f0_rising` = 672510 Hz), which
   the testbench documents as "the gain peaks back over unity and the
   single-crossover phase-margin reading is not the whole story".

  other for a phase that is physically the same to within 3 °. A ~4 % shift in
  the resonance frequency is enough to flip that.
- Both designs carry the same 0.6 … 0.9 MHz resonance, above 0 dB, at these
  points. Its **0 mA** rows — which both records call FAIL either way — put
  it on the record explicitly: `fs_-40c_3.63v` / 0 mA / 0.33 µF / 1 mΩ reads
  `f_180` = 809.7 kHz, GM = +32.09 dB in the superseded record and 845.8 kHz,
  GM = +25.52 dB here.

**Mechanism of the shift.** Issue #55 adds exactly two devices to this loop's
AC path, `Mclamp_bg` and `Mclamp_bg_ss` — 10 µm/0.5 µm PMOS from `VIN` to
`BG`, gated by their cells' `CLG`, which rests at `VIN` in every steady state
this sweep solves. They are **off** at every one of the 3240 operating points;
their only AC contribution is ≈ 5 fF of drain junction each on `BG`, a node
whose own capacitance is ≈ 0.75 pF (dominated by `Mbuf`'s 150 µm/1 µm gate).
That is a ~1.3 % perturbation of one node, and it moves the resonance by ~4 %
— which is what flips 57 of 3240 dec-50 verdicts.

**Conclusion this record draws, and the one it declines to draw.** It draws:
the light-load 0.6 … 0.9 MHz resonance is a property of #51's gate buffer,
present in both designs, and the dec-50 grid cannot report it reliably — a
1.3 % capacitance change on one node moves 57 verdicts while moving every
phase margin by under half a degree. It declines to draw: that this design is
worse there. Where a grid fine enough to see the feature was run, this design
measured better at the point in question and no worse at the others.

The resonance itself is issue **#53**'s and the grid resolution is issue
**#58**'s; neither is touched here.

Read together: the worst phase margin degrades non-monotonically with load current,
while the 0 dB crossover frequency moves over
384.9 Hz - 2.578e+06 Hz across
the matrix. Crossover rising with load current is the pass device's gm
rising with its drain current; phase margin falling as it rises means
the loop is meeting poles that do not move with load. The external ESR
zero cannot rescue the 1 mOhm end -- that zero sits at ~500 MHz there and
supplies no phase lead in band -- which is why the 1 mOhm column is the worst ESR column at every load.

Note that the a-priori worst point named in issue #10 and in
`spec/architecture-survey.md` section 4.1 -- light load, minimum C_eff,
no ESR zero, i.e. {0mA, 0.33uF, 0.001 ohm} --
**FAILS** here (worst-corner PM 11.54 deg at
`ff_125c_2.97v`), whereas the heaviest-load column is where the
matrix fails (worst-corner PM 179.02 deg at
50mA / 4.7uF / 0.001 ohm,
`ff_125c_2.97v`).
The classic light-load argument assumes a crossover that does not move
much with load; this loop's does, because the pass device's gm -- and
with it the loop's unity-gain frequency -- rises with load current
while the poles that eat the phase do not move with it. So the load
axis runs the *opposite* way to the a-priori expectation, even though the a-priori point fails on its own account here too: the matrix is short of margin along its whole length, not just at one end of it.

## What this record asks of the next design step

This is a measurement of `design/ldo_core.sch` **as committed** --
the transistor-level `design/error_amp.sch` and the
`design/ldo_ilimit.sch` limit block, both real. No behavioural
amplifier stands in for anything, so the failures tabulated above are
a property of this compensation as designed, not an artifact of a
placeholder.

That is not a contradiction of the amplifier's own record: the
amplifier was sized against the offset, PSRR and quiescent-current
budgets (`sim/amp-openloop/records/`, `sim/psrr-vs-freq/records/`,
`sim/quiescent-current/records/`), and closing the LDO loop around it
is a separate requirement that nothing has yet been sized against.
This record is the first measurement of that requirement, and it is
what the compensation step has to be designed against.

DR-0001 called the mechanism in advance: "with no minimum ESR, phase
margin must come from pole placement, not from the external zero: the
pass-gate pole `p2 = 1/(2*pi*Rgate*Cgate)` must sit well above
crossover at the 0.33 uF corner". Issue #10's own guidance says a
broad failure of this shape is a compensation problem to fix in the
design, not something this testbench should work around -- so this
record tunes nothing to make the matrix pass.

The concrete, measured requirement it hands to the compensation step
is therefore: with the loop's non-dominant poles where they are, the
crossover frequencies tabulated above must be brought below them (or
the poles above the crossovers) -- crossover reaches 2.58e+06 Hz
somewhere in this matrix and is 1.11e+06 Hz at the
worst-margin point itself -- by enough margin to leave PM >= 45 deg at
every point of the matrix, the 1 mOhm ESR column included. The levers
are the amplifier's Miller network (`XCc`/`XRz` in
`design/error_amp.sch`), a lower-impedance pass-gate drive, or a lower
loop unity-gain frequency (`spec/architecture-survey.md` section 5,
candidates 2 and 3). Re-running this same sweep against the
recompensated loop mints the record that supersedes this one.
