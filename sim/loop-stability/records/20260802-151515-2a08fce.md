# Record 20260802-151515-2a08fce

- **Record ID**: 20260802-151515-2a08fce
- **Claim**: `spec/decision-records/DR-0001-output-cap-strategy.md` -- stability criterion "worst-corner phase margin
  >= 45 deg **and** gain margin >= 10 dB across the full matrix",
  verified over the ratified `I_load` x `C_eff` x `ESR` x PVT matrix
  (issue #10). This is the stability evidence for the LDO loop; per #10's
  scope note it is *the* phase-margin record, and #12's spec-line testbench
  suite should reference it rather than duplicate a phase-margin testbench.
- **Netlist provenance**: schematic (`design/ldo_core.sch`, via
  `design/netlist/ldo_core.spice`).
  The DUT is the **real** loop: `design/error_amp.sch` (transistor-level,
  Miller-compensated) and `design/ldo_ilimit.sch` (the real limit block), with
  the pass device and the 300k/600k feedback divider from
  `design/ldo_core.sch`. No behavioural amplifier is used. The only
  idealizations in the loop are the ones the other records already name:
  `Vref1`, an ideal 1.2 V source standing in for a bandgap block that does not
  exist yet, and `Fbias` inside `ldo_ilimit`. The load is an ideal DC current
  source (see "Operating conditions"), which is the conservative choice for a
  stability measurement.
- **Corner matrix run**:
  - Process: ff, fs, res_ff, res_ss, sf, ss, tt
  - Temperature: -40, 27, 125 degC
  - Supply: 2.97, 3.30, 3.63 V
  - 63 PVT points x 6 load x 3 cap x 4 ESR
    = **4536 loop-gain points** (the full
    4536-point
    grid DR-0001 (plus issue #54's `res_ff`/`res_ss` process corners)
    enumerates, when run with the defaults).
- **Operating conditions**:
  - Load current: 0mA, 0.1mA, 1mA, 10mA, 25mA, 50mA, modelled as an ideal DC
    current source (infinite small-signal impedance -- the conservative LDO
    stability model; a resistive load would add damping in parallel with the
    output pole and flatter the result). 0 mA means no external load, i.e.
    only the feedback divider's ~6 uA, per DR-0001.
  - Output cap: C_eff in {0.33uF, 1uF, 4.7uF} with
    ESR in {0.001, 0.05, 0.2, 0.5} ohm, in series, at the output
    pin. These are DERATED EFFECTIVE values per DR-0001, not nominal
    component values. The 1 mOhm point is the numerical stand-in for
    "ESR -> 0" (no ESR zero to help), which DR-0001 makes a required point.
  - Enable state: enabled (EN = VIN), steady state.
  - Loop break: `ERRAMP_OUT` -> `PASS_GATE`, the two deliberately-unconnected
    ports `design/ldo_core.sch` exposes. Tian/Middlebrook dual injection
    (series voltage + shunt current), so the reported loop gain is exact
    regardless of the source/load impedance ratio at the break, and the DC
    operating point is the true closed-loop one (the injection source is a
    DC short).
  - Capless operation is **not** swept: DR-0001 scopes it out of the primary
    design as a separate fork.
- **Statistical convention**: N/A (corner-matrix claim, not a distribution
  claim -- no Monte Carlo).
- **Result**:
  - **Overall: FAIL** against `spec/decision-records/DR-0001-output-cap-strategy.md`
    (1099/4536 points pass, 3437 fail). This verdict is
    the DR-0001 phase/gain-margin criterion and nothing else; the DR-0008
    precondition check below is reported as its own, separate verdict.
  - **Worst point**: `res_ss_-40c_2.97v` at
    I_load = 50mA, C_eff = 0.33uF,
    ESR = 0.001 ohm --
    **phase margin -97.29 deg** (bar: >= 45 deg),
    gain margin -23.63 dB (bar: >= 10 dB),
    crossover 1.06e+06 Hz, DC loop gain 139.5 dB.
  - **Best point** (for range): `ff_125c_3.63v` at
    1mA / 4.7uF / 0.5 ohm --
    phase margin 121.29 deg.
  - Phase margin across the whole matrix spans
    -97.29 deg to 121.29 deg.
  - **Both** of DR-0001's bars are broken here, and by the same mechanism: 3369/4536 points miss the >= 45 deg phase-margin bar and 2381/4536 miss the >= 10 dB gain-margin bar. A negative gain margin means the phase reaches -180 deg while |T| is still above 0 dB -- the -180 deg crossing sits *below* the 0 dB crossing rather than above it. Only 0/4536 points have no -180 deg crossing in 0.01 Hz - 1 GHz at all. This record reports the two Bode margins DR-0001 asks for and nothing beyond them: a closed-loop stability verdict would need a Nyquist reading, which is not attempted here and is not what the ratified criterion is written in terms of.

while the 0 dB crossover frequency moves over
358.6 Hz - 2.31e+06 Hz across
the matrix. Crossover rising with load current is the pass device's gm
rising with its drain current; phase margin falling as it rises means
the loop is meeting poles that do not move with load. The external ESR
zero cannot rescue the 1 mOhm end -- that zero sits at ~500 MHz there and
supplies no phase lead in band -- which is why the 1 mOhm column is the worst ESR column at every load.

Note that the a-priori worst point named in issue #10 and in
`spec/architecture-survey.md` section 4.1 -- light load, minimum C_eff,
no ESR zero, i.e. {0mA, 0.33uF, 0.001 ohm} --
**FAILS** here (worst-corner PM 10.72 deg at
`ff_125c_2.97v`), whereas the heaviest-load column is where the
matrix fails (worst-corner PM -97.29 deg at
50mA / 0.33uF / 0.001 ohm,
`res_ss_-40c_2.97v`).
The classic light-load argument assumes a crossover that does not move
much with load; this loop's does, because the pass device's gm -- and
with it the loop's unity-gain frequency -- rises with load current
while the poles that eat the phase do not move with it. So the load
axis runs the *opposite* way to the a-priori expectation, even though the a-priori point fails on its own account here too: the matrix is short of margin along its whole length, not just at one end of it.

## Issue #54: does the resistor-corner axis introduce a new failure?

Issue #54's premise: since #51/#56 recompensated the loop as a Type-II gain
shelf, `Rz` (a `ppolyf_u_1k` resistor) sets both the shelf gain and the shelf
corner frequency, so its process spread — `sim/devchar/CONCLUSIONS.md` S2
puts `ppolyf_u_1k` at ~40% ff/ss — is a first-order input to this record, not
a second-order one, and it was previously unverified: every prior record held
`res_*` at typical throughout. This record adds `res_ff`/`res_ss` to the
process axis (5 -> 7 corners, 3240 -> 4536 points) to close that gap. Its own
acceptance criteria: report the resistor-corner result honestly, and if
`res_ff`/`res_ss` breaks a point the MOS-only (`tt`/`ff`/`ss`/`fs`/`sf`)
matrix passes, call that a FAIL and hand it to a design issue rather than
retuning `Rz` (or anything else) to make it pass.

**Measured: the resistor-corner axis introduces zero new failures.** Grouping
this record's 4536 rows by the non-process axes (temp x supply x load x C_eff
x ESR = 648 distinct operating points) and comparing each point's 5-MOS-corner
verdict against its 2-resistor-corner verdict:

| | count | of those, `res_ff`/`res_ss` disagrees |
|---|---|---|
| all 5 MOS corners PASS | 136 | **0** flip to FAIL under `res_ff`/`res_ss` |
| all 5 MOS corners FAIL | 471 | 0 flip to PASS under `res_ff`/`res_ss` (no rescue either) |
| mixed across the 5 MOS corners | 41 | (not applicable to this specific criterion) |

Zero points in the first row means the acceptance criterion's FAIL-and-file
branch does not trigger: there is no case here where `Rz`'s process spread,
by itself, breaks a configuration the MOS-skew axis would have called clean.
The pass-rate comparison across the whole matrix reinforces this: MOS-only
rows pass 785/3240 = **24.2284%**, `res_ff`/`res_ss` rows pass 314/1296 =
**24.2284%** — identical to 4 significant figures. The resistor-corner axis
does not shift where the overall frontier sits.

That is a **conditional** result, not a blanket "`Rz` spread doesn't matter"
finding — conditional on the same broad failure this record's matrix already
reports (785/3240, `sim/loop-stability/README.md` and DR-0009), which leaves
little room for a resistor corner to be the deciding factor at any given
point: 471/648 operating points already fail at every MOS corner before
`res_ff`/`res_ss` is even considered, so most of the matrix has no "MOS
passes" baseline for a resistor corner to break in the first place. The 136
operating points that do pass under all 5 MOS corners are the only ones this
criterion can meaningfully test, and none of them flip. Once the compensation
changes (the architecture-level fix DR-0009 recommends, since `#53`'s
super-source-follower and isolated-bias-reference candidates were both
rejected per DR-0010/DR-0011 and only the adaptive pass-device-replica-bias
candidate remains untried), this comparison should be re-run against the
wider passing set that fix is expected to produce — a resistor-corner effect
invisible against a 24% baseline may not stay invisible against a much larger
passing region.

`res_ff`/`res_ss` **do** appear disproportionately at the tails of this
record's per-corner tables above: the single worst point in the whole matrix
is `res_ss_-40c_2.97v` (PM -97.29 deg, GM -23.63 dB) versus the worst MOS
corner `sf_-40c_2.97v` (PM -94.57 deg, GM -22.50 dB) — both at the same
50 mA / 0.33 uF / 0.001 ohm configuration that fails regardless of process
corner, so this is the resistor spread modestly widening an already-failing
tail, not creating a new failure mode. `res_ff` corners are also the best
performers in a few 0 mA / light-load cells (e.g. `res_ff_125c_2.97v` is the
worst-corner reference for the 0 mA / 4.7 uF rows above). Both directions are
consistent with `Rz` doing exactly what DR-0009 says it does — setting the
gain-shelf's gain and corner frequency — rather than introducing a distinct
mechanism.

## What this record asks of the next design step

This is a measurement of `design/ldo_core.sch` **as committed** --
the transistor-level `design/error_amp.sch` and the
`design/ldo_ilimit.sch` limit block, both real. No behavioural
amplifier stands in for anything, so the failures tabulated above are
a property of this compensation as designed, not an artifact of a
placeholder.

That is not a contradiction of the amplifier's own record: the
amplifier was sized against the offset, PSRR and quiescent-current
budgets (`sim/amp-openloop/records/`, `sim/psrr-vs-freq/records/`,
`sim/quiescent-current/records/`), and closing the LDO loop around it
is a separate requirement that nothing has yet been sized against.
This record is the first measurement of that requirement, and it is
what the compensation step has to be designed against.

DR-0001 called the mechanism in advance: "with no minimum ESR, phase
margin must come from pole placement, not from the external zero: the
pass-gate pole `p2 = 1/(2*pi*Rgate*Cgate)` must sit well above
crossover at the 0.33 uF corner". Issue #10's own guidance says a
broad failure of this shape is a compensation problem to fix in the
design, not something this testbench should work around -- so this
record tunes nothing to make the matrix pass.

The concrete, measured requirement it hands to the compensation step
is therefore: with the loop's non-dominant poles where they are, the
crossover frequencies tabulated above must be brought below them (or
the poles above the crossovers) -- crossover reaches 2.31e+06 Hz
somewhere in this matrix and is 1.06e+06 Hz at the
worst-margin point itself -- by enough margin to leave PM >= 45 deg at
every point of the matrix, the 1 mOhm ESR column included. The levers
are the amplifier's Miller network (`XCc`/`XRz` in
`design/error_amp.sch`), a lower-impedance pass-gate drive, or a lower
loop unity-gain frequency (`spec/architecture-survey.md` section 5,
candidates 2 and 3). Re-running this same sweep against the
recompensated loop mints the record that supersedes this one.
