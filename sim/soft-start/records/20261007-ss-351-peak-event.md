## Verdict

**The lower peak is not a real improvement. It is a time-step artifact of the
`tran 1u 9m` measurement.** Resolved properly, both DUTs have the same peak,
about **296 mA** peak `Icap` and **395 V/ms** peak `dV/dt`, at the same
instant (151.82 us), from the same event. DR-0037 must not cite the 142 mA /
99 V/ms numbers, and nothing here supports a claim that the servo reduces
startup inrush.

## Where and when the peak happens

The enable edge is at 100-101 us. The peak is **not** at the edge. It is at
**t = 151.82 us, 51 us after EN**, in both DUTs, and lasts ~2 us.
Before it, from the EN edge to ~150 us, `Icap` is below 0.02 mA: the
soft-start block holds `PASS_GATE` (and `BG`) at VIN through `Mhold_ss` /
`Mhold_bg_ss`, whose gate `HG` follows EN through a slow RC
(`Rh_ss` / `Ch_ss`, ~50 us to the hold threshold). The peak sequence, identical in both DUTs
(`event_new.csv`, `event_baseline.csv`):

| t (us) | event |
|---|---|
| 150.56 | `HG` has reached ~2.5 V (|Vsg| of `Mhold_ss` at its threshold); `PASS_GATE` starts to leave VIN |
| 150.74 | `PASS_GATE` < 3.0 V; the error amplifier is still far from regulation (`FB` = 1.08 V < `VREF`), so its output (`BG`) slews low with nothing opposing it |
| 151.18 | `PASS_GATE` < 2.5 V: the pass device is on, `Icap` > 5 mA |
| 151.52 | `PASS_GATE` < 2.0 V, `Icap` ~ 100 mA and climbing ~0.65 mA/ns |
| 151.80 | `PASS_GATE` = 1.26 V, `Icap` = 277 mA; the `ldo_ilimit` comparator output `CO` has risen, `CLG` starts to fall |
| 151.82 | peak: `Icap` = 296 mA (supply 298 mA), `dV/dt` = 395 V/ms, `PASS_GATE` = 1.2 V |
| 151.84 | `Mclamp` has pulled `PASS_GATE` and `BG` back to VIN in < 20 ns; `Icap` falls to the load/ESR level (-1.8 mA); `Vout` steps down ~27 mV (the ESR drop across 0.1 ohm leaves) |

So the peak is the **gate slew at hold-release**: the hold is let go before
the loop is in regulation, the error amplifier output slews freely, and the
pass device is turned on hard until the **current-limit clamp** trips
(a sub-microsecond comparator-plus-clamp delay) and cuts it off. The peak height is
set by how far `PASS_GATE` falls before the clamp acts; the ~2 us width means
the peak is the top of a fast triangular pulse, not a plateau. It is not
caused by the soft-start ramp (`SSR`, which is still at ~0 V at 152 us), nor
by the EN edge itself.

The blocks that set this event are the same in both DUTs: the hold RC, the
error amplifier (`design/error_amp.sch`, unchanged by DR-0037) and the
`ldo_ilimit` threshold (`VTH`, set by `VREF/Rbias` current, which DR-0037
reproduces to ~6 ppm at this corner per its own consequences section). The
waveforms agree: `HG`, `PASS_GATE`, `BG`, `CO`, `CLG`, `VTH` match to the
third digit through the whole event (`event_*.csv`).

## Why the two DUTs read 142 vs 282 mA

The peak is a ~2 us, steeply rising pulse that the clamp truncates. A
transient with `tran 1u 9m` has a 1 us maximum step, and the solver picks its
own (smaller) steps in the pulse by local truncation error. How high the
sampled maximum goes then depends on exactly which steps land in the last
200 ns before the clamp. The two netlists have different node counts and
different LTE histories (the servo adds ~20 nodes), so the same circuit event
is sampled differently. Evidence, peak vs max time step on the same decks
(`convergence.csv`, one corner, tt/27 C/3.30 V):

| max step | new peak Icap (mA) | baseline peak Icap (mA) | new dV/dt (V/ms) | baseline dV/dt (V/ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| solver's own, `tran 1u 9m` (`peak_probe.py --native`) | 269.2 | 278.0 | 368 | 343 |
| 100 ns | 278.8 | 246.7 (peak at 152.9 us) | 316 | 276 |
| 20 ns | 276.7 | 276.7 | 376 | 376 |
| 5 ns | 292.9 | 293.2 | 393 | 394 |
| 2 ns | 295.5 | 295.5 | 395 | 395 |
| 1 ns | 296.0 | 296.0 | 395 | 395 |

and through `klt sim` (`corners/20261007-ss-351-peak-tt27/summary.csv`,
`tran 2n 400u`): new 295.89 mA, baseline 295.89 mA, 394.93 vs 394.93 V/ms,
both at 151.819 us. The original driver, rerun here unchanged
(`corners/20261007-ss-351-rerun-1u/summary.csv`), reproduces the reported
142.19 mA / 281.64 mA / 99.15 / 368.99 V/ms exactly: the numbers are
deterministic but under-resolved. Note the 1 us-step numbers are not even
monotone in step size (the baseline reads 247 mA at 100 ns), so neither
"142" nor "282" is a converged value of the peak; the converged value is
~296 mA for both, and the reading error of the original driver is
-52 % (new) / -5 % (baseline) at this point.

The peak is also 4-5x the 62 mA worst-corner current limit that the
"startup stays >= 10 mA below the limit" clause is written against, in the
baseline as well. That is the pre-existing known gap that DR-0006 records
(`README.md`, "The known gap"); this record neither changes nor relaxes it.

## What this means for DR-0037

- The servo does not change the startup peak at tt / 27 C / 3.30 V.
  The 2-3.7x lower peak in `20261007-ss-320-tt27-probe` is withdrawn as a
  result of the new bias; DR-0037's "Open" bullet for #351 can be closed on
  this explanation once the cross-corner check below exists.
- The ramp slope result of that record (2.036 vs 2.057 V/ms) is unaffected:
  the slope is taken between 0.4 V and 1.4 V of Vout, long after the event.
- `icap_peak_ma`, `isup_peak_ma` and `dvout_max_vpms` from `tran 1u 9m`
  (both `run.sh`'s and `klt_run.py`'s) should be read as a lower bound with
  an error that depends on the netlist, not as a comparable number between
  netlists. A peak-event measurement needs a max step <= ~5 ns across the
  window (error < 1 % vs 1 ns); `klt_peak_run.py` is that measurement and is
  the driver to use for the DR-0006 inrush and clearance clauses. This
  record does not modify `run.sh`, `klt_run.py` or `summarize.py`, nor any
  earlier record or the spec.
