DR-0023's claim about what it fixes, and it is why a seventh sizing sweep of
the mirror is not the next move.

## 3. Why the acquisition transient got worse: it is T2, measured

Inrush at C_eff = 1 µF went from 18/79 clean to 0/83 clean. Two pieces of
evidence say this is the cascode *un-masking* an existing defect rather than
creating a new one.

**(a) The clean points, before, were all at high supply.** Same bench, same
C_eff = 1 µF / 36 Ω points, binned by supply:

| Vin | uncascoded clean (≤ 5 mA) | cascoded clean |
|---|---|---|
| 2.97 V | 0 / 21 | 0 / 21 |
| 3.30 V | 7 / 20 | 0 / 21 |
| 3.63 V | 8 / 19 | 0 / 21 |

High supply is exactly where DR-0023's table measured the uncascoded mirror's
ΔV_ds — and therefore its over-injection — to be largest (+1.35 µA of excess
at `tt_27c_3.30v`, +2.54 µA at `tt_27c_3.63v`, against +0.45 µA at
`tt_27c_2.97v`).

**(b) The arithmetic of the hold release.** At the instant `Mhold_ss` lets go
of `PASS_GATE`, the loop's DC target is

```
VOUT(target) = 1.5 · VREF − I_inj(V(SSR)=0) · Rtop
             = (6.00 µA − I_inj(V(SSR)=0)) · 300 kΩ
```

which is T2's error expressed as a voltage. Cascoded, `I_inj(0)` is
4.985…5.807 µA — **below 6.00 µA at all 63 corners** — so that target is
always positive, **+58 … +305 mV**, and it has to be acquired into C_out
through a 2000 µm pass device in about a microsecond (1 µF × 0.2 V / 1 µs =
200 mA). Uncascoded, over-injection made the same target *negative* at the
high-supply corners: the pass device simply stayed off and there was nothing
to acquire. Those points were not clean because the element was accurate —
they were clean because it was wrong in the direction that hides this edge,
at the price of T5 at 37/63 corners.

The correlation between the predicted step and the measured peak is weak
(Pearson r = 0.19 over the 63 main-matrix points), and that is the point: the
peak is **slew-limited by the loop and the pass device, not step-limited**.
The step decides *whether* there is an acquisition; the loop decides how
violently. That is also the answer to `design/softstart_injection_
compensation.md` §4's open arithmetic (a 2.1× difference in the voltage to be
acquired producing a 356× difference in the current) — the two quantities are
not proportional and were never expected to be once the acquisition is
slew-limited.

**Nothing that leaves T2 unmet can fix this.** Inside T2's own ±0.10 µA the
acquisition step is at most ±30 mV, which is the level the ideal `Binj_ss`
element demonstrated clean.

## 4. The named ideal-ramp A/B (`softstart_injection_compensation.md` §4)

The experiment §4 names, run: `V(SSR)` forced by an ideal PWL source through
the `SSR` port issue #196 promoted, against the same point's own
internally-generated ramp. The PWL is fitted **per point** to that point's own
`internal` arm (two fixed `V(SSR)` crossings, 0.2 V and 1.0 V, turned into a
slope, a time origin and the arm's own settled ceiling), so the two arms
traverse the same trajectory and the A/B isolates the ramp node's *dynamics
and source impedance*, not its rate. `Fss_ramp`, `Css`, `Mdis_ss` and
`Mtop_ss` are present and connected in both arms; an ideal source simply
absorbs whatever they push.

Driver: `sim/soft-start/testbench/ramp_ab.py`; deck:
`tb_ss_ramp_ab.spice.in`; logs and rollup:
`corners/20260918-190207-8a59d23-rampab/`.

| corner | fitted slope | `icap_peak` internal | `icap_peak` ideal | Δ | `VOUT` at 700 µs, internal → ideal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tt_-40c_2.97v` | 1388.1 V/s | 269.92 mA | 245.75 mA | **−9.0%** | 1.1907 → 1.1906 V |
| `tt_-40c_3.30v` | 1388.5 V/s | 272.42 mA | 283.15 mA | **+3.9%** | 1.1478 → 1.1477 V |
| `tt_-40c_3.63v` | 1388.8 V/s | 253.39 mA | 285.22 mA | **+12.6%** | 1.1025 → 1.1025 V |
| `tt_27c_2.97v` | 1563.6 V/s | 232.65 mA | 230.30 mA | −1.0% | 1.3132 → 1.3132 V |
| `tt_27c_3.30v` | 1564.0 V/s | 230.24 mA | 228.60 mA | −0.7% | 1.2742 → 1.2741 V |
| `tt_27c_3.63v` | 1564.3 V/s | 202.90 mA | 198.25 mA | −2.3% | 1.2328 → 1.2328 V |

**Result: conclusive, and negative for the ramp-node hypothesis.** Replacing
the entire ramp generator's output impedance and dynamics with an ideal
source moves the acquisition peak by −9.0%…+12.6% — two orders of magnitude
short of accounting for a 5 mA bound missed by 40–60×. `t_startup` agrees to
within 0.1 µs on every point and `VOUT` at 700 µs to within 0.1 mV, so the
two arms really are traversing the same trajectory and the null result is a
measurement rather than a mis-fit.

The ramp node is therefore ruled **out** as the mechanism, which is what §4
asked for. Combined with §3's arithmetic, what is left is the hold-release
step itself, and that step is T2.

## Same-binary A/B: cascoded vs. uncascoded, one host, one binary

`sim/soft-start/testbench/run.sh` gained an `LDO_NETLIST` override in this

"Pre-existing #191 regression" section had to. The `B` side is
