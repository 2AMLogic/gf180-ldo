The deck's ideal current sink had a second, unphysical solution for the t=0 operating point in which the pass device is off and the sink holds VOUT tens of volts BELOW ground, drawing its current through forward-biased substrate junctions in the PDK device models; a cold Newton-Raphson start reached that solution at most corners and poisoned the whole transient. The sink is now compliance-limited (see tb_load_transient.spice and sim/harness/README.md, 'Compliance-limited load sinks'): it draws its commanded current for VOUT well above ground and nothing at or below ground, which is what any real load does, and which deletes the unphysical solution rather than biasing the solver away from it. iload_err_max_ua below takes the worst deviation of the delivered load current from the commanded load current over EVERY timepoint of the 400 us run and checks it under 1 uA, so 'the compliance bound never engaged' is a directly measured per-corner fact here rather than an assumption or a voltage-envelope proxy. vout_min_global_v reports how deep the excursion actually went; its 0.5 V floor is derived from the compliance factor itself (1 - f = 6.2e-6 there, i.e. 0.31 uA at 50 mA), not picked as a round number.
