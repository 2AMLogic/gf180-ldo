## Old-vs-new

No earlier record of this bench measured the current DUT (`4f99a1a9…`; the DR-0037 servo, #320), so there is no same-DUT comparison. Against `20260923-141156-33035d7` (previous DUT, local run, open_pdks `c6d73a3`) the two differ in DUT, backend and PDK revision together; the PDK revision alone moved nothing (batch `c6d73a3` vs batch `f6eeac7`: byte-identical `summary.csv`, and a single-corner local run reproduces the batch values to every printed digit).

On the 22 corners that pass, `vdrop_mv` agrees with the old record to within 0.0013 mV, `vout_at_210_mv` to within 0.004 mV, and `iload_ma` / `knee_uniqueness_mv` exactly. Five corners moved out of tolerance and are **not** relaxed: `ff_-40c_2.10v`, `fs_27c_2.10v`, `ss_27c_2.10v`, `sf_27c_2.10v`, `res_ff_27c_2.10v` fail the `knee_uniqueness_mv` guard (259-323 mV against +/-0.001). The first falling crossing of 1.764 V sits at Vin = 2.185-2.190 V, the top of the 2.20 -> 1.75 V sweep, so `vdrop_mv` reads 420-426 mV at those corners; the last crossing is at the real knee (1.86-1.93 V). That is the artifact the guard was added for (a sweep point that lands on a non-physical solution inserts a spurious crossing pair): those five corners have **no valid dropout reading**, and this record does not claim one for them. `vout_at_210_mv` (1799.2-1799.5 mV) and `iload_ma` (50 mA) are valid at all 27 corners, so the ratified Vin = 2.10 V test point is still met everywhere.
