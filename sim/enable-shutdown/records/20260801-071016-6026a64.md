## Result: no regression on the row this bench owns

| Quantity | before #38 | after #38 | ratified |
|---|---|---|---|
| shutdown Iq @ ff/125 °C/3.63 V | 0.2026 µA | **0.2037 µA** | < 3 µA |
| Vin→Vout leakage @ same corner | 0.2143 µA | **0.2143 µA** | ≤ 1 µA |
| disabled `VOUT` @ same corner | 0.1796 V | **0.1796 V** | pass device off, no active discharge |
| `NBIAS` disabled, all corners | 0.000 V | **0.000 V** | — |
| enabled Iq @ ff/125 °C/3.63 V | 22.38 µA | **24.11 µA** | < 30 µA |
| enabled Iq, best corner | 9.44 µA | 9.94 µA | — |

**Shutdown Iq moves by 1.1 nA — 0.55%.** Issue #38's clause 5 asked
specifically that a soft-start ramp not be built from a resistor to the rail,
because that is the easy way to lose this row; `ldo_softstart` has no such
resistor, and its common-source stage's load resistor sits behind an EN-gated
PMOS switch precisely because the block's disabled state parks its clamp gate
*low*. This measurement is that design decision's evidence. **Leakage and the
disabled output state are bit-identical** to the previous record at the binding
corner: the soft-start block adds nothing to the disabled circuit but device
leakage.

**Enabled Iq rises 1.73 µA** (7.7%), to 24.11 µA against the ratified < 30 µA —
a 5.9 µA margin where there was 7.6 µA. The increase is the soft-start
comparator's tail (~1.4 µA) plus its `VREF`/`Rss_bias` branch (~0.4 µA); the
common-source stage contributes ~0 in the settled enabled state, because its
output node ends up at `VIN` with the pull-down off, so its load resistor
carries no current once the ramp is over.

## Transient section: the enable→active transition, now soft-started

Taken over the widened window and therefore **not** directly comparable to the
previous record's transient numbers, except for peak supply current and
overshoot, which are extrema over the whole enable window in both records:

| Quantity | before #38 | after #38 |
|---|---|---|
| peak supply current, 50 mA startup | 153.4 … 289.1 mA | **53.2 … 138.9 mA** |
| startup overshoot | 1.8339 … 1.9173 V | **1.8034 … 1.9082 V** |
| enable → 1.764 V | 27.97 … 63.66 µs | 2289 … 5764 µs |

Peak supply current is lower at **all 63 corners**. Overshoot is lower at 59
and higher at 4, all of them in the ff / res_ff family.

## The ff / res_ff family was already ringing, and still is

`vout_pp_late` — peak-to-peak output ripple in a window taken after settling —
reads **0.1786 V** at `res_ff_125c_2.97v` in the *pre-#38* record and
**0.2160 V** at the same corner here. The pre-#38 number is measured over a
50 µs window and this one over 400 µs, so the two are not a like-for-like
amplitude comparison, but the pre-#38 number is by itself proof that the main
loop was not settling at that corner before this issue touched anything. Nine
corners in this run flag "regulation dipped below 1.764 V after settling", all
in the same family.

Main-loop compensation is issue #10's subject. This record notes the behaviour,
attributes the four corners where overshoot got worse to it, and does not claim
either to have caused it or to have fixed it. The full-window, capacitor-swept
picture — including the DR-0001 0.33 µF / near-zero-ESR corner, where the
oscillation is unmistakable — is in
`sim/soft-start/records/20260801-071013-6026a64.md`.
