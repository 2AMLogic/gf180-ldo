off. Enabled Iq is **0.53 µA lower**, consistent with

cut when it stopped driving the 6.14 pF pass gate, and less than that was
spent in the buffer.

## Regression, stated plainly: the startup transient

The DC rows above are clean. The **transient** section of this same run is
not, and this record reports it rather than confining itself to the row it
owns:

| Quantity | before #51 | after #51 | ratified row it bears on |
|---|---|---|---|
| enable → 1.764 V (`t_startup_us`) | 2289 … 5764 µs | **27.3 … 2015 µs** | Startup: controlled ramp ≤ 1 V/ms |
| peak supply current, 50 mA startup | 53.2 … 138.9 mA | **141.1 … 366.0 mA** | Startup: inrush ≤ 5 mA at 4.7 µF |
| startup overshoot (`vout_max_en`) | 1.8034 … 1.9082 V | **1.8421 … 1.9746 V** | Startup: overshoot ≤ +2 % (1.836 V) |
| corners with overshoot > 1.836 V | 9 / 63 | **63 / 63** | same |
| post-settling ripple (`vout_pp_late`) | 0.0000 … 0.2160 V | **0.0806 … 0.2969 V** | Startup / Load transient |
| corners where the limit clamp engages while settled at 50 mA | 6 / 63 | **54 / 63** | Current limit: never engages for I_load ≤ 50 mA |

**Root cause, measured.** `t_startup_us` collapsing from milliseconds to tens
of microseconds is the tell: **the soft-start ramp is being bypassed.** Both
`design/ldo_softstart.sch` and `design/ldo_ilimit.sch` act on the regulator by
*pulling `PASS_GATE` up* — they were sized against the drive strength that
node had before #51, which was a class-A stage sinking ~4 µA. #51 put a PMOS
source follower on that node whose sink capability is not a 4 µA current
source but a 150 µm/1 µm device that conducts as hard as its Vsg allows. Both
clamps are therefore overpowered: the loop wins the node, the output ramps at
the main loop's own speed instead of the soft-start ramp's, the inrush that
the ramp existed to bound reappears (366 mA peak), and the resulting
pass-current overshoot trips the current limit — which is what puts
`clg_min_reg` down at 0.09 V at 54 corners.

This is *not* a small-signal stability problem. The same DUT, at the same
50 mA / 1 µF / 100 mΩ operating point this transient uses, measures a phase
margin of 161–189° in
`sim/loop-stability/records/20260801-191742-84f67b8.md`. The ringing here is a
large-signal limit cycle between the main loop and a clamp that can no longer
hold the node, not a loop that has lost margin.

**Two probes bounding the fix**, run against this same DUT (exploratory, not
recorded as evidence — `NO_RECORD=1`, tt / 27 °C / 2.97 V only, where this
record measures `vout_pp_late` = 0.185 V and `clg_min_reg` = 2.152 V):

- Strengthening the buffer's static sink `Mpgn` 4× (and its pull-up with it):
  `vout_pp_late` 0.205 V, `clg_min_reg` 2.156 V — **no improvement**. The
  static balance at the node is not the mechanism.
- Speeding the buffer's pull-up by taking `Rbufb` from 5 MΩ to 500 kΩ:
  `vout_pp_late` 0.150 V, `clg_min_reg` **2.876 V** — the limit clamp stops
  engaging at that corner, and overshoot drops from 1.909 V to 1.890 V. So
  the asymmetry between the follower's fast pull-down and the replica
  pull-up's `Rbufb`-limited response is a real contributor. It is not a
  drop-in fix: `Rbufb` is what keeps `N1 → Mbufb → PASS_GATE` from being a
  feedforward path around the compensation, and shortening it trades directly
  against the loop-stability result (measured with `Rbufb` absent entirely:
  worst PM −167°).

The clean fix is more likely structural — move the soft-start and
current-limit clamps from `PASS_GATE` to the buffer's *input* node `BG`, so
they steer the buffer instead of fighting it — but that changes two more cells
and needs its own full re-verification. It is filed as its own issue rather
than attempted here.
